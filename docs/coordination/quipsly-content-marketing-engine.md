# Quipsly content marketing engine

Date: 2026-06-14
Owner: Codex / Product Owner lane
Status: Strategic direction, with subdomain/hub taxonomy still WIP

## Purpose

This document preserves Charlie's dream for how Quipsly should market itself:

Quipsly should use its own content pipeline to create genuinely useful learning content for the kinds of creators, researchers, teachers, and makers it serves.

The marketing should not be a bait funnel. It should be proof of the product philosophy.

## Two founding motivations

### 1. Systems anxiety relief

Quipsly exists to help people overcome systems anxiety, attention fragmentation, and creative overwhelm.

For Charlie, this is personal:

- ADHD should be harnessed, not treated as a character flaw.
- Quipsly should act like a prosthetic attention span.
- The system should make capture, organization, research, creation, production, publishing, and learning feel possible instead of terrifying.

This is not just positioning. It is product truth.

### 2. Earned educational marketing

Quipsly exists as an excuse to make genuinely helpful, inspiring, practical content.

The model is closer to StudioBinder's best educational content than to shallow SaaS lead magnets:

- Teach the craft for real.
- Help the audience become the kind of creator the software supports.
- Make the content valuable even if the viewer never buys.
- Let the product be a natural next step, not the hidden toll booth.

Google's people-first content guidance is also aligned with this: create primarily for people, not search-engine manipulation.

## Marketing gospel

1. We do not drip just enough knowledge to bait people.
2. We do not pander to advertisers.
3. We do not make generic SEO sludge.
4. We do not turn every article into a disguised sales page.
5. We do create content that is genuinely useful without purchase.
6. We do show the product as the workshop that made the thing.
7. We do teach complete workflows, not isolated tricks.
8. We do create creators, researchers, teachers, editors, writers, and makers who are stronger because they found us.
9. We do use our own pipeline to prove that the pipeline works.
10. We do make the motive obvious: Quipsly is useful, so we can afford to be generous.

## Strategic pattern

Each audience hub should eventually have this shape:

1. `Teach`: high-quality learning content for the craft.
2. `Show`: examples built with Quipsly.
3. `Template`: reusable packets, checklists, scripts, prompts, documents, or workflows.
4. `Tool`: a Quipsly workbench that helps do the job.
5. `Publish`: example outputs pushed through the Quipsly pipeline.
6. `Learn`: analytics, feedback, and iteration notes showing what improved.

The content itself becomes the product demo.

## Recommended hub/subdomain strategy

Do not launch twenty subdomains immediately.

Start with internal hub vocabulary, then promote a few proven hubs to public subdomains when they deserve their own identity.

Suggested stages:

### Stage 1: internal hub routes

Use routes like:

- `quipsly.com/hubs/write`
- `quipsly.com/hubs/pod`
- `quipsly.com/hubs/research`
- `quipsly.com/hubs/story`
- `quipsly.com/hubs/photo`

This avoids DNS/content sprawl while we discover what works.

### Stage 2: flagship subdomains

Promote only the strongest/clearest audiences:

- `write.quipsly.com`
- `pod.quipsly.com`
- `research.quipsly.com`
- `story.quipsly.com`
- `photo.quipsly.com`
- `teach.quipsly.com`

### Stage 3: specialized verticals

Only once the content/workbench pairing is real:

- `ml.quipsly.com`
- `ai.quipsly.com`
- `marketing.quipsly.com`
- `quotes.quipsly.com`
- `storyboard.quipsly.com`
- `familyhistory.quipsly.com`

## Content packet model

Every major public piece should be created as a Quipsly packet, not just as a blog post.

Suggested packet shape:

- `audience`
- `jobToBeDone`
- `pain`
- `learningGoal`
- `sourceMaterials`
- `outline`
- `draft`
- `examples`
- `quotes`
- `mediaAssets`
- `transcript`
- `publishedOutputs`
- `templates`
- `analytics`
- `lessonsLearned`
- `nextRepurposingIdeas`

This makes the marketing engine a live demonstration of Quipsly.

## Example: Pod hub

Content idea:

`How to turn one recording into a full episode page, short clips, quotes, and social posts without losing your mind.`

The piece should include:

- full guide article;
- video walkthrough;
- downloadable planning template;
- example transcript;
- clip decisions;
- YouTube publish packet;
- episode page packet;
- social quote cards;
- Patreon post draft;
- analytics follow-up after publication.

The product demo is not separate. The guide is built from the actual pipeline.

## Example: Coaching hub

Context:

Homer recently completed ICF Level 2 coach training in Amsterdam in May 2026, and has real contacts in the coaching world. Coaching is not a side niche. It is a core wedge.

Strategic idea:

Coaching skills are leadership skills. They are also a plausible add-on service for creators who already teach, publish, lead communities, run memberships, or help audiences change.

Content idea:

`How to turn your expertise into a coaching session people can actually use.`

The piece should include:

- coaching session structure;
- discovery questions;
- session notes template;
- client follow-up packet;
- action plan;
- transcript example;
- quote/excerpt extraction;
- ethical boundaries;
- content repurposing into article, clip, email, Patreon post, or course lesson.

Product proof:

Quipsly can capture sessions, transcribe them, organize insights, create follow-up materials, connect coaching to content, and preserve the human context.

Important:

Do not pretend Quipsly replaces coach training, ethical judgment, or human presence. The product helps coaches prepare, capture, organize, follow up, and turn hard-won insight into reusable resources.

Marketing is a core wedge because Quipsly itself needs to learn marketing in public, because Homer has contacts from his coaching certification circle, and because creator businesses need publishing, repurposing, analytics, and social workflows.

The Marketing hub should help us get better at marketing while we teach it. That humility is an advantage. We should not pretend to be gurus.

## Voice and tone

Quipsly content should feel:

- generous;
- specific;
- a little weird in a human way;
- practical;
- non-patronizing;
- craft-respecting;
- anti-systems-anxiety;
- clear about what the product does without constantly selling.

Avoid:

- guru voice;
- fake certainty;
- "10 hacks" sludge;
- vague thought leadership;
- shaming people for using AI;
- withholding the actual useful part.

## UX implications

The marketing dream changes product UX:

1. Every public content piece should be traceable back to a Quipsly source packet.
2. The publishing UI should show where a piece has been distributed.
3. The editor should make repurposing obvious: article, script, video, quote, course, social, podcast page.
4. Analytics should feed lessons learned back into the content packet.
5. Nests should be able to hold a whole content campaign, not just a document.
6. The product should make "make the helpful thing" easier than "make the sales funnel."
